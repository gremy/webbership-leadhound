Who to contact

{{if eq $v.Section "who"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

The countries LeadHound may write to. A lead in a country that is left out never joins a new batch.

More about this

The countries listed are the ones the leads settings file names, each with its own channel and language, shown under In the file and on the server. Leads from a country the file does not name are never written to anyway; the box below makes that rule explicit, and each such lead says "never contacted".

These countries are kept with the fallback order, but each section saves and resets on its own: resetting this one leaves the fallback order as it is.

Lead kinds

{{if eq $v.Section "kinds"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

The kinds of lead LeadHound drafts, such as a site on a Drupal version past its end of life. Turn a kind off to stop drafting it, and use Up and Down to choose which kind new batches draw first.

More about this

The order gives each kind its points in the fallback order: the first kind gets the most, 10 points more than the next. If the fallback order sets its own points for kinds, those decide instead, and a note at the top of this page says so.

Starred leads always come first, whatever their kind. Up and Down save straight away, with the ticks as they are on screen.

Small sites

{{if eq $v.Section "small-sites"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

Leave out sites too small to be worth an email. Each site has a rank from the public top-sites lists it was found on, where 1 is the busiest site.

More about this

A site left out stays on the Leads list, marked "small site", so you can still look at it, but it leaves the queue and new batches. Raise the number, or set 0, to bring those leads back.

It applies only to leads that come from a site on those lists; other kinds of lead are never left out by it.

A rank, for example 100000: that keeps the top 100,000 sites and leaves out sites ranked lower or not ranked at all. 0 is off, the default.

Batches draw from

{{if eq $v.Section "batches"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

Where a new batch takes its leads from. A batch is a set of drafts you read and approve before anything is sent.

More about this

For example, with 5 starred leads and room for 20 in a batch: Starred leads only drafts those 5 and stops. Starred leads first, then the rest drafts the 5, then 15 more from the queue in the fallback order, or by relevance once relevance has passed its check.

With Starred leads only and no starred lead ready, a new batch drafts nothing and says so. How many leads a batch holds is under Sending pace and ramp, below.

Jev relevance

{{if eq $v.Section "relevance"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

Jev reads each lead and gives it a relevance number from 0 to 100: how likely the site is to want the work. The number shows on Leads; it orders the queue only once it has passed its check, below.

More about this

A lead is rated once, and again only when what Jev reads about it changes, such as its version or an end-of-life date. Rating costs little; the daily limit is there to stop a run that goes wrong, not to keep to a budget.

On by default. Turned off, no lead is rated; leads keep the numbers they already have.

Email check

{{if eq $v.Section "email-check"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

While the Email check is on in Controls, every address is checked with Reoon before it can enter a live batch. Some domains accept mail for any name, so their addresses cannot be confirmed: these are catch-all addresses.

More about this

Catch-all domains are common among small businesses, and many of their addresses are real and read. Reoon advises sending to them in small batches and watching bounces; a mailbox pauses itself on too many bounces. Invalid, disabled, throwaway, full and trap addresses are always left out; an address Reoon cannot answer for is checked again the next day.

Fallback order

{{if eq $v.Section "order"}}{{template "settings_problems" $v}}{{end}}

How leads that are not starred are ordered while relevance has not passed its check. A lead scores the sum of the points below, and higher scores come first.

More about this

Starred leads always come first, in your own order. Once relevance passes its check, it orders the leads that are not starred, and these points decide among equal numbers and among leads not rated yet.

Leads with the same score go bigger site first. A change applies to the queue straight away and to the next new batch. Countries never to contact are under Who to contact.

{{with $x.Order.Points}}
{{range .}}
{{end}}

Points from -100 to 100 for each kind, Drupal version and channel. A minus number pushes those leads down, and a blank box is no rule.

{{end}}

Points for a site whose exact version is known, such as 7.98 rather than just 7. From -100 to 100; blank is no rule.

For example 10000 15, 100000 8: a site in the top 10,000 earns 15, one in the top 100,000 earns 8. A site earns the first band its rank is under, and an unranked site earns none.

For example GB 20, DE 10. A country not listed scores 0.
